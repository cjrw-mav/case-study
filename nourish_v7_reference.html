Cross-cutting tensions

Product scalability

Feature velocity matters while finding product-market fit. At scale, ICP, unit economics, portfolio discipline, adoption and technical debt need to stay connected.

Unused features are expensive inventory.

Enterprise push and pull

Enterprise and public-sector requirements can absorb material security, compliance, integration and technical capacity.

How far do we want enterprise requirements to reshape the product and operating model?

Scaling go-to-market

Experimentation needs to sharpen into clearer ICP, repeatable playbooks, expansion motion, account ownership and intentional coverage design.

Org design should follow economics and customer value — not convention.

Knowledge scalability

Knowledge must move from local tribes and individual heads into a shared system for ownership, information and organisational learning.

Tribal knowledge → institutional knowledge.
Systems matter more than heroics as you scale.

The important thing is not predicting exactly which problem appears at £50m or £80m. It is building an operating and data model now that can evolve with the business.

Operating philosophy

Five principles that guide how I would approach the role.

Customer value tells us what matters. Missions organise the work. Systems make it repeatable. Technology creates leverage. Culture determines whether any of it sticks.

01

Start with customer value

Understand how value is created before optimising the organisation around it.

02

Organise around missions and outcomes, not functions

Give important cross-functional missions one accountable owner and bring the necessary disciplines together around a shared outcome.

Functions provide expertise. Missions provide direction.
03

Optimise the system, not individual functions

The biggest constraints often sit between teams — handoffs, dependencies, incentives, queues and information flow.

04

Use technology to release human capacity

AI and automation should create more space for judgment, creativity, relationships and care.

05

Add structure without losing humanity

Standardise where variation creates friction or risk; preserve autonomy where it creates speed, learning, ownership and innovation.

Section 1 · First 90 days

Hypothesis-led diagnosis across the whole operating system.

The aim is not seven independent audits. It is to understand how the system creates customer value, where scale is creating constraints and which problems matter most.

Output

Operational Leverage Agenda

A fact-based 12-month view of the highest-value enterprise priorities.

30 / 60 / 90 progression
Days 1–30

UNDERSTAND

Listen · Observe · Map

  • Meet and get to know key stakeholders and their business areas.
  • Conduct Gemba walks externally with customers and internally with key teams to understand how they work.
  • Review the key tools, systems and processes currently in use.
  • Review what AI has been put in place to date.
  • Discuss the results and lessons from the hackathon.
  • Review past performance data, employee survey results and other existing sources of organisational insight.
  • Observe as many meetings and discussions as possible — customer meetings and demos, but just as importantly internal meetings and decision forums.
Build an evidence base around how Nourish actually works, rather than relying on the documented version of how it is supposed to work.
Days 31–60

DIAGNOSE

Test · Quantify · Prioritise

By this point there will inevitably be a lot of information. The job is to determine whether the biggest constraints and bottlenecks are becoming visible — or whether the obvious symptoms are still masking them.

  • Trace constraints and bottlenecks across the whole value chain.
  • Understand the root causes, their impact and where those bottlenecks really live.
  • Test how those constraints could be solved rather than simply treating symptoms.
  • Understand and quantify the capacity that could be unlocked.
  • Begin to develop and rank opportunities within the Operational Leverage Map.
Move from a large body of observations to a smaller number of evidence-backed enterprise constraints.
Days 61–90

MOBILISE

Align · Design · Start

Where the evidence is very clear, some interventions may already be underway. For the larger pieces:

  • Go ahead and install the new mechanisms required to address the constraint.
  • Agree the programmes that need to run.
  • Establish ownership, outcomes and measures.
  • Begin the visible interventions.
Day 90 should mark the point where the operating system is starting to change — not the point where diagnosis finally produces a report.

Prioritisation

Operations should not become a queue of whoever shouts loudest.

Potential initiatives should be assessed consistently against customer, business and capacity outcomes.

Customer impact

How materially does this improve customer experience or outcomes?

Business impact

Growth, retention, margin, risk or strategic progress.

Capacity released

Does it remove recurring work or unlock scarce expertise?

Strategic relevance

Does it materially advance a company-level priority?

Cost of delay

What happens if this constraint remains in place?

Feasibility

Effort, dependencies, adoption, risk and reversibility.

Prioritisation is ultimately an allocation of scarce organisational capacity.

Section 2 · Delivering on it

Build enough central capability to optimise the enterprise — not another silo.

The goal is not to pull every Operations role into one organisation. It is to create the connective tissue that helps the enterprise work as one system.

What I need

Sponsorship + decision rights

Clear sponsorship, access to people, customers and data, permission to challenge existing norms, and the ability to stop work rather than only add more.

How it scales

Small core, federated network

A small Enterprise Ops nucleus working through Product Ops, RevOps, Customer Ops, People, Data/AI and functional leaders rather than creating another empire.

Biggest risks

Dependency + adoption

An unbounded mandate that turns Ops into the fixer of everything; and structure that exists on paper but never becomes organisational behaviour.

Centralise the mechanisms that optimise the enterprise; keep specialist operations close to the functions.

The throughline

Technology should give people more capacity to do the work only people can do.

Operational leverage is not the end goal. It is the mechanism that allows Nourish to serve more customers, create better experiences and ultimately support better care at greater scale.

Less friction
More human capacity

Better care
Sustainable growth

My job would not be to build Operations for Operations' sake. It would be to build an operating system that allows Nourish's people to spend more time creating value for customers, use technology where it genuinely improves that system, and preserve what makes Nourish special as it scales.
